CLASSICAL STATEMENTS (from the standard literature; this is your only mathematical input)

Notation for CaseyFuss1: for two circles with centres P_i, P_j and radii r_i, r_j
(r >= 0; radius 0 = a point), the length of the DIRECT (exterior) common tangent is
  sqrt(|P_iP_j|^2 - (r_i - r_j)^2)
and the length of the TRANSVERSE (interior) common tangent is
  sqrt(|P_iP_j|^2 - (r_i + r_j)^2),
whenever the quantity under the root is >= 0.

CaseyFuss1 (Casey's theorem; generalised Ptolemy).
 (a) [Most common textbook form] Let Gamma be a circle with centre O and radius R.
     Let C_1, C_2, C_3, C_4 be four circles (radii r_i >= 0, point-circles allowed)
     lying inside Gamma, each internally tangent to Gamma, at points K_1, K_2, K_3, K_4
     which occur in this cyclic order around Gamma, and with no two of the C_i
     intersecting (i.e. pairwise non-overlapping). Let t_ij be the length of the
     direct common tangent of C_i and C_j. Then
         t_12 * t_34 + t_14 * t_23 = t_13 * t_24.
     (When all r_i = 0 this is Ptolemy's theorem for the cyclic quadrilateral
     K_1K_2K_3K_4.)
 (b) [General / signed-radii form] Let Gamma be a circle with centre O and radius R > 0.
     Let C_1,...,C_4 be circles, each tangent to Gamma at K_i, with K_1,K_2,K_3,K_4 in
     this cyclic order on Gamma (pairwise distinct). Give C_i the signed radius
     rho_i = +r_i if C_i touches Gamma internally (lies inside Gamma) and
     rho_i = -r_i if C_i touches Gamma externally (lies outside Gamma), so that
     |O O_i| = R - rho_i. Define
         t_ij = sqrt( |O_iO_j|^2 - (rho_i - rho_j)^2 ),
     i.e. t_ij is the direct common tangent if C_i, C_j touch Gamma in the same way and
     the transverse common tangent if they touch Gamma in opposite ways. Then
         t_ij = |K_iK_j| * sqrt((R - rho_i)(R - rho_j)) / R,
     and
         t_12 * t_34 + t_14 * t_23 = t_13 * t_24.
 (c) [Converse] Conversely, if four circles C_1,...,C_4 satisfy
         t_12 * t_34 +- t_13 * t_24 +- t_14 * t_23 = 0
     for a suitable choice of signs, where each t_ij is a direct or transverse
     common tangent chosen consistently as in (b) (all direct; or the three
     tangents from one circle transverse and the others direct; or direct within
     two pairs and transverse between them), then the four circles are all
     tangent to a fifth circle or to a straight line.

CaseyFuss2 (Fuss's theorem for bicentric quadrilaterals).
 (a) Let ABCD be a convex quadrilateral that is bicentric, i.e. it has a
     circumcircle (centre O, radius R) and an incircle (centre I, radius r).
     Let x = |OI|. Then
         1/(R - x)^2 + 1/(R + x)^2 = 1/r^2,
     equivalently
         (R^2 - x^2)^2 = 2 r^2 (R^2 + x^2),
     equivalently x^2 = R^2 + r^2 - r*sqrt(4R^2 + r^2).
     Consequence: R >= sqrt(2) * r, with equality iff x = 0 (the square).
 (b) [Converse] If two circles, of radii R and r with the circle of radius r
     lying strictly inside the circle of radius R and with centre distance x,
     satisfy 1/(R - x)^2 + 1/(R + x)^2 = 1/r^2, then there exists a convex
     quadrilateral inscribed in the larger circle and circumscribed about the
     smaller one.

CaseyFuss3 (Poncelet closure for bicentric quadrilaterals).
 (a) [Poncelet's porism, quadrilateral case] Let Gamma (radius R) and omega
     (radius r) be two circles with omega lying strictly inside Gamma. If there
     exists one quadrilateral inscribed in Gamma and circumscribed about omega
     (a bicentric quadrilateral with these circles), then for EVERY point P on
     Gamma there is a quadrilateral with P as a vertex that is inscribed in Gamma
     and circumscribed about omega; it is obtained by drawing from P a tangent to
     omega, meeting Gamma again at the next vertex, and repeating — the polygonal
     path closes after exactly four steps. In particular there are infinitely many
     such quadrilaterals.
 (b) [Existence criterion, combining (a) with CaseyFuss2] For circles as in (a)
     with centre distance x, a bicentric quadrilateral with circumcircle Gamma and
     incircle omega exists (and then one through every point of Gamma) if and only
     if 1/(R - x)^2 + 1/(R + x)^2 = 1/r^2.
