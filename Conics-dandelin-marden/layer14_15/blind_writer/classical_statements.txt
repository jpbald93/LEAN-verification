CLASSICAL STATEMENTS (from the standard literature; this is your only mathematical input)

Setting for Conics1-Conics3: Euclidean 3-space. A (right circular, double) cone K has
apex V, axis a line L through V, and half-opening angle alpha with 0 < alpha < pi/2;
K is the union of all lines through V making angle alpha with L. A plane P not through V
makes angle beta (0 <= beta <= pi/2) with the axis L. The section P ∩ K is
  an ellipse    if beta > alpha (P meets only one nappe, in a closed curve; beta = pi/2 gives a circle),
  a parabola    if beta = alpha (P parallel to exactly one generator),
  a hyperbola   if beta < alpha (P meets both nappes).

Conics1 (Dandelin spheres, ellipse).
 Let P be a plane not through V with beta > alpha, so that E = P ∩ K is an ellipse.
 (a) There are exactly two spheres S1, S2 that are each tangent to the cone K along a
     circle (C1, C2 respectively) and tangent to the plane P (at points F1, F2
     respectively); they lie inside the same nappe, on opposite sides of P.
 (b) F1 and F2 are the foci of E: for every point X of E,
         |X F1| + |X F2| = constant,
     the constant being the length of the segment of any generator of K between the
     circles C1 and C2 (equivalently, the major axis 2a of E).

Conics2 (Dandelin spheres, hyperbola).
 Let P be a plane not through V with beta < alpha, so that H = P ∩ K is a hyperbola
 (two branches, one on each nappe).
 (a) There are exactly two spheres S1, S2, one inside each nappe, each tangent to K along
     a circle (C1, C2) and tangent to P (at F1, F2).
 (b) F1 and F2 are the foci of H: for every point X of H,
         | |X F1| - |X F2| | = constant,
     the constant being the length of the segment of any generator between C1 and C2
     (equivalently, the transverse axis 2a of H).

Conics3 (Focus-directrix property via Dandelin spheres; includes the parabola).
 Let P be a plane not through V, Q = P ∩ K a non-degenerate section (ellipse, parabola or
 hyperbola, not a circle). Let S be a Dandelin sphere: tangent to K along a circle C lying
 in a plane P_C, and tangent to P at F. (For the parabola, beta = alpha, there is exactly
 one such sphere.) Let d = P ∩ P_C (a line, since P is not parallel to P_C when Q is not a
 circle).
 (a) For every point X of Q,
         |X F| = e · dist(X, d),   where   e = cos(beta) / cos(alpha),
     a constant (the eccentricity); F is a focus and d the corresponding directrix.
 (b) Consequently e < 1 for the ellipse, e = 1 for the parabola (|XF| = dist(X,d): the
     parabola is the locus of points of P equidistant from F and d), e > 1 for the hyperbola.
 (c) (Converse, planar form.) Given in a plane a line d, a point F not on d, and e > 0, the
     locus {X : |XF| = e · dist(X,d)} is an ellipse if e < 1, a parabola if e = 1, a
     hyperbola if e > 1, with F as a focus and d as the corresponding directrix.

Setting for Conics4-Conics5: the complex plane C identified with the Euclidean plane;
 a, b, c in C are the vertices of a non-degenerate triangle (not collinear).

Conics4 (Steiner inellipse: existence and uniqueness).
 (a) There is a unique ellipse inscribed in the triangle abc (i.e. tangent to all three
     side lines, lying in the closed triangle) that is tangent to each side at its midpoint.
 (b) Its centre is the centroid g = (a+b+c)/3. Among all ellipses inscribed in the
     triangle it is the one of maximal area (area = pi/(3 sqrt 3) · area(abc)).

Conics5 (Marden's theorem; Siebeck 1864).
 Let p(z) = (z - a)(z - b)(z - c), with a, b, c the non-collinear vertices of a triangle.
 Then the two roots of p'(z) (counted with multiplicity) are the foci of the Steiner
 inellipse of the triangle. (If p' has a double root, the inellipse is a circle centred
 at it; this happens iff the triangle is equilateral.)
 Equivalently, the foci f1, f2 satisfy f1 + f2 = 2(a+b+c)/3 and f1 f2 = (ab+bc+ca)/3.
 (General Siebeck form, not required: if the inscribed ellipse is tangent to the sides at
 the points dividing them in ratios m1:m2, etc., its foci are the roots of
 m1/(z-a) + m2/(z-b) + m3/(z-c) = 0; Marden's theorem is m1 = m2 = m3.)
