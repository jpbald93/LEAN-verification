CLASSICAL STATEMENTS (from the standard literature; this is your only mathematical input)

Notation: a triangle ABC is non-degenerate (A, B, C not collinear); a = BC, b = CA, c = AB,
s = (a+b+c)/2 the semiperimeter, r the inradius, I the incenter.

B1. FEUERBACH'S THEOREM.
  Let ABC be a triangle. Its nine-point circle (the circle through the midpoints of the three
  sides, the feet of the three altitudes, and the midpoints of the segments joining the
  orthocenter to the vertices) is tangent to the incircle and to each of the three excircles.
  (a) The nine-point circle is internally tangent to the incircle (the incircle lies inside the
      nine-point circle). Equivalently, with N the nine-point centre and R the circumradius,
      NI = R/2 - r.
  (b) The nine-point circle is externally tangent to each of the three excircles. Equivalently,
      for the excircle with centre I_A and radius r_A opposite A, N I_A = R/2 + r_A (and
      likewise for B, C).
  Remark: if ABC is equilateral, the nine-point circle coincides with the incircle (NI = 0,
  R/2 = r); otherwise the point of contact in (a) is unique (the Feuerbach point).

B2. MIQUEL'S THEOREM (triangle / "pivot" theorem).
  Let ABC be a triangle and let D, E, F be points on the sides BC, CA, AB respectively
  (most commonly: on the side lines, distinct from the vertices). Then the three circles
  through A, E, F; through B, F, D; and through C, D, E pass through a common point M
  (the Miquel point).
  (Where one of the triples is collinear, the "circle" is to be read as the line through
  those points; with D, E, F distinct from the vertices and on the open segments, each of
  the three triples is non-collinear and the circles are genuine circles.)

B3. BRAHMAGUPTA'S FORMULA.
  Let ABCD be a (convex) cyclic quadrilateral, i.e. its four vertices lie on a circle, with
  side lengths a = AB, b = BC, c = CD, d = DA, and semiperimeter s = (a+b+c+d)/2. Then its
  area K is
      K = sqrt( (s-a)(s-b)(s-c)(s-d) ).
  Equivalently 16 K^2 = (-a+b+c+d)(a-b+c+d)(a+b-c+d)(a+b+c-d).
  (Letting d -> 0 recovers Heron's formula. For a general convex quadrilateral, Bretschneider:
  K^2 = (s-a)(s-b)(s-c)(s-d) - abcd cos^2((A+C)/2), which reduces to B3 iff A + C = pi.)

B4. MALFATTI CIRCLES.
  (a) Existence. In every triangle ABC there exist three circles lying inside the triangle,
      each tangent to the other two (externally) and each tangent to two sides of the
      triangle: the circle C_A tangent to sides AB and AC, C_B tangent to BA and BC, C_C
      tangent to CA and CB. (These three circles are uniquely determined by these conditions.)
  (b) Radii (Malfatti's formula). Let d = IA, e = IB, f = IC be the distances from the
      incenter to the vertices. Then the radii r_1, r_2, r_3 of the Malfatti circles near
      A, B, C respectively are
        r_1 = r / (2(s-a)) * ( s - r + d - e - f ),
        r_2 = r / (2(s-b)) * ( s - r - d + e - f ),
        r_3 = r / (2(s-c)) * ( s - r - d - e + f ).
