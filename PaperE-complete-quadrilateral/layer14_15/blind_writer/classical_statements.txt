CLASSICAL STATEMENTS (from the standard literature; this is your only mathematical input)

Setting — the complete quadrilateral. Four lines in the Euclidean plane, in general position (no two parallel, no three through one point). Each pair meets in a vertex (six vertices). They form four triangles (each obtained by omitting one line). The three diagonals are the segments joining the three pairs of opposite vertices (vertices not on a common line among the four).
 E1 (Newton–Gauss line, Gauss 1810). The midpoints of the three diagonals are collinear (and not all equal).
 E2 (Steiner 1828 / Bodenmiller). The orthocentres of the four triangles are collinear (the orthocentre line), and they are not all the same point.
 E3 (Gauss–Bodenmiller). The three circles having the three diagonals as diameters are coaxal: each orthocentre has the same power with respect to all three circles (so the orthocentre line is their radical axis).
 E4. The orthocentre line is perpendicular to the Newton–Gauss line.
 E5 (Miquel, 1838). The circumcircles of the four triangles pass through one common point M (the Miquel point).
 E6 (Steiner circle). The four circumcentres of the four triangles and the Miquel point M lie on one circle.
 E7 (Simson line of the quadrilateral). The feet of the perpendiculars from M to the four lines are collinear.
 E8 (Steiner). That line (E7) is parallel to the orthocentre line (E2).
