## Issues

1. **MAJOR — The rectangular-hyperbola explanation is mathematically false.** Paper lines **341–344**, with related introductory wording at **85–89**.

   “The rectangular hyperbolas through four points in general position form a pencil, and their common centre is the Poncelet point” confuses the ordinary pencil of conics through four points with its rectangular members. For a non-orthocentric quadrangle there is generally just one rectangular conic up to scaling, not a pencil of rectangular hyperbolas. In the orthocentric case there is a pencil, but its centres are not one common point: they range over the common nine-point circle. This is exactly the exceptional geometry discussed later in the draft.

   Independent certificate: write a rectangular conic as `A(x²−y²)+Bxy+Cx+Dy+E=0`. Evaluation at `(0,4,1+2i,3+4i)` gives a rank-4 matrix with one-dimensional nullspace, generated by `(8/23,5/23,−32/23,1,0)`. For the paper's orthocentric example the rank is 3 and the nullspace is spanned by `(-1/4,-7/8,1,0,0)` and `(0,-1,0,1,0)`. The latter family plainly does not have a fixed centre. The Brianchon–Poncelet article itself explicitly explains the infinitely many hyperbolas and indeterminate centre in the orthocentric exception (after Theorem VIII).

   **Fix:** say that the pencil of conics through the four points has, in the non-orthocentric case, a unique rectangular member, possibly a degenerate perpendicular pair of lines; its centre motivates the formula. State the orthocentric exception separately. Alternatively delete the pencil assertion and simply give the algebraic derivation under a nonzero denominator. Do not imply that `QGen` alone guarantees a nondegenerate rectangular hyperbola. No Lean theorem needs changing.

2. **MAJOR — The prior-art sweep missed a directly relevant published formal theorem; its broad negative verdict needs correction.** Paper lines **63–65, 249–250, 506–536**; `reports/priorart.md`, especially the claim that the triangle Steiner line was not found anywhere.

   I found and inspected **“Steiner's Line Theorem in Isabelle/HOL,” Arthur Freitas Ramos, David Barros Hulak, Ruy Jose Guerra Barretto de Queiroz, AFP, 6 October 2026**:
   - Entry: https://isa-afp.org/entries/Steiner_Line_Theorem.html
   - Checked statement/source presentation: https://isa-afp.org/browser_info/current/AFP/Steiner_Line_Theorem/Steiner.html
   - `steiner_line` proves collinearity of the three sideline reflections of a circumcircle point.
   - `steiner_line_through_orthocenter` proves that these reflected-point lines contain the triangle orthocentre.
   - Definitions are the same coordinate geometry: reflection `2*foot−m`, orthocentre `a+b+c−2*ctr`; hypotheses are noncollinearity, circle memberships, and positive radius.

   This is **not** a complete formal proof of the four-line theorem in Paper F, so I do not classify it as FOUND-COMPLETE for that theorem. It is, however, directly relevant formal partial/ingredient prior art: apply it to the four triangles and their common Miquel point, then identify the lines using the distinct reflected points. The paper's claim of finding nothing “complete or partial” and the sweep's triangle-Steiner negative result cannot remain without qualification. A scoped first proof of the full four-line formulation may still be defensible; this discovery does not refute all novelty claims.

   **Fix:** add an AFP citation and a FOUND-PARTIAL/ingredient verdict for A1, distinguish full quadrilateral statements from the already formalised triangle theorem, and rerun/update the relevant prior-art searches. Prefer “we found no earlier formalisation of the full statements, subject to these gaps” to “these are the first formal proofs” at line 533. The four main gaps are otherwise faithfully repeated, but the report explicitly lists **GeoGebra/Coq exports** as uncovered; add that named gap too. Do not generalise “not kernel-checked” to every synthetic/Wu-method implementation: the same sweep records HOL Light's kernel-checked Gröbner Simson example.

4. **MINOR — Non-orthocentricity is introduced too late in the abstract and introduction.** Paper lines **56–62 and 85–89**.

   The abstract first says that for four points with no three collinear the defined point lies on four nine-point circles and three circles meet only there. Those claims need `d ≠ ortho a b c`, not just `QGen`. The later exception helps a careful reader but does not make the initial statement exact. In the orthocentric example, the totalised Lean formula gives `d=1+3i/2`, whose squared distance to the common nine-point centre is `41/64`, not its radius squared `65/64`; the claimed membership is actually false for that definition. The introduction also speaks of “one point” and “the rectangular hyperbola” before stating the exception.

   **Fix:** begin the Part-B abstract sentence “For a non-orthocentric complete quadrangle ...” and qualify the introductory description similarly. Retain the separate orthocentric-coincidence result.
