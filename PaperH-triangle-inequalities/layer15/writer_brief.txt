CLASSICAL STATEMENTS (standard literature: Bottema et al., Geometric Inequalities; Johnson, Advanced Euclidean Geometry; Coxeter–Greitzer; Mitrinović–Pečarić–Volenec). Triangle ABC non-degenerate in a real Euclidean space; sides a = BC, b = CA, c = AB; semiperimeter s; area Δ; circumcentre O, circumradius R; incentre I, inradius r; excentre I_A and exradius r_A opposite A; centroid G; orthocentre H.
 E1 (Euler). OI² = R² − 2Rr.
 E2 (Euler's inequality). R ≥ 2r, with equality iff the triangle is equilateral.
 E3 (Feuerbach–Euler for excircles). OI_A² = R² + 2R r_A (and similarly for B, C).
 E4. OG² = R² − (a²+b²+c²)/9; OH² = 9R² − (a²+b²+c²); IG² = (s² + 5r² − 16Rr)/9.
 E5 (Fundamental inequality / Blundon's quartic). s⁴ − 2(2R² + 10Rr − r²)s² + r(4R+r)³ ≤ 0.
 E6 (Gerretsen). 16Rr − 5r² ≤ s² ≤ 4R² + 4Rr + 3r².
 E7 (Kooi). s² ≤ R(4R+r)² / (2(2R−r)).
 E8 (Weitzenböck). a²+b²+c² ≥ 4√3 Δ, equality iff equilateral.
 E9 (Hadwiger–Finsler). a²+b²+c² ≥ 4√3 Δ + (a−b)² + (b−c)² + (c−a)², equality iff equilateral.
 E10 (Isoperimetric inequality for triangles). (a+b+c)² ≥ 12√3 Δ, equality iff equilateral.
 E11 (Leibniz). For every point X: XA² + XB² + XC² = 3XG² + (a²+b²+c²)/3; so the sum is minimised exactly at X = G.
 E12 (Neuberg–Pedoe). For triangles with sides a,b,c, area Δ and a′,b′,c′, area Δ′: a′²(b²+c²−a²) + b′²(c²+a²−b²) + c′²(a²+b²−c²) ≥ 16ΔΔ′, equality iff the triangles are similar (a′:b′:c′ = a:b:c).
 P1 (Ptolemy's inequality). For any four points A,B,C,D: AC·BD ≤ AB·CD + BC·AD.
 P2 (Ptolemy's theorem and converse). For four distinct points, equality holds iff A,B,C,D lie in this order on a circle or a line.
 M1 (Erdős–Mordell). For a point X inside or on triangle ABC with distances p, q, r to the side lines BC, CA, AB: XA + XB + XC ≥ 2(p + q + r), equality iff the triangle is equilateral and X is its centre.
 M2 (Barrow). For X inside ABC, if the internal bisectors of angles BXC, CXA, AXB meet BC, CA, AB at U, V, W, then XA + XB + XC ≥ 2(XU + XV + XW).
 F1 (Fermat–Torricelli). If every angle of ABC is less than 120°, there is a point F seeing each side under 120°, and F minimises XA + XB + XC over all points X.
 F2 (Fermat–Napoleon). In the plane, erect equilateral triangles BCA′, CAB′, ABC′ outward on the sides. Then AA′ = BB′ = CC′; the lines AA′, BB′, CC′ are concurrent; if all angles are < 120° they meet at the Fermat point F and AA′ = FA + FB + FC; and the lines meet at 60° angles.
 F3 (Napoleon). The centres of these three equilateral triangles form an equilateral triangle whose centre is the centroid of ABC.
