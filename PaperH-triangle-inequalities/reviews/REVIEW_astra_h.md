## Numbered findings

1. **MINOR — lines 68–72 and 636–641: make the angle restriction explicitly govern the Fermat identification.** The numbered Theorem 20 is correct, but the abstract's new sentence “In the oriented plane we prove ... pass through the Fermat point, with AA'=FA+FB+FC” can be read as unconditional; the same restriction is implicit in the paragraph after Theorem 21. The lines are concurrent and have the stated direction angles for every triangle, but their common point is a minimiser only under the stated <120° hypothesis. For A=(0,0), B=(6,0), C=(-1,1), angle A=135°; the common point is approximately (-0.1569321,-0.3059443), whereas the minimiser is A, and |AA'|≈7.3751139 is not the minimum 6+sqrt(2)≈7.4142136. **Exact fix:** say “the segments AA', BB', CC' have equal lengths and their supporting lines are concurrent and meet pairwise at π/3; if all three triangle angles are <2π/3, their common point is the Fermat point and |AA'|=FA+FB+FC.” Start line 636 with “Under the angle hypotheses of (iii), ...”. This also avoids assigning finite lengths to lines.

2. **MINOR — lines 677–679 and 735–742: distinguish proof dependence from mathematical necessity in the ablation column.** “binders needed/unused” is stronger than the experiment establishes. The actual gapcheck.py replaces one hypothesis proposition by True and recompiles the existing proof; failure means this proof did not survive the change, not that the weakened theorem is false. The paper itself acknowledges at 376–378 a potentially redundant hypothesis in kooi_of_quartic, despite its being counted among the 29 “needed” binders. **Exact fix:** change the legend to “hypothesis ablation: existing proof fails/survives after replacing one hypothesis by True”, and add “This tests dependence of the supplied proof, not logical necessity of a hypothesis.” Retain the numerical counts. The separate unused-variable-linter result is fine.

3. **MINOR — lines 169–170: the claim that area is the only new EulerIneq definition besides s is literally false.** EulerIneq/Bridges.lean:25 defines gram and :29 defines sideLen; both are genuine noncomputable definitions and are included in REQUIRED. This is only an API-description issue, not a theorem error. **Exact fix:** replace the opening with “Besides the semiperimeter, the main geometric definition introduced in EulerIneq is the area”; optionally add “The auxiliary definitions gram and sideLen package the Heron polynomial and the opposite-side indexing.”

4. **MINOR — line 261, Table 1: “six distinct points hyp.” misstates the hypothesis.** There are four points, with six pairwise disequalities, as the theorem body correctly says at line 453. **Exact fix:** replace the table text by “four pairwise distinct points (six disequalities)”.
