## Mathematics checked independently

1. **Signature and tangency signs.** The source form is `−B`; diagonalising the v,w block gives one positive and one negative direction, hence source signature (n+1,2) and B signature (2,n+1). Direct symbolic expansion gives
   
   `LHS(tangency) − RHS(tangency) = −v'² B(X,X) − v² B(Y,Y)`.
   
   The repaired defect formula is correct. In particular, for equal **nonzero raw v-slots** v=v′=t, the t² cancels on both sides and the paper's `2B=‖c−c′‖²−(r−r′)²` is correct. I do **not** adopt the contrary extra `/t²` suggestion in L13_STATEMENTS s07. If c,r denote affine rather than raw coordinates, they must first be divided by t.

2. **Explicit determinant link.** Let M have rows `(r,a,b,s,1)`, `s=a²+b²−r²`, q=cofactor(M). For `P=(q₀,−q₁,−q₂,2q₃,−q₄)` and `C=(r,a,b,1,−s/2)`, exact expansion gives
   
   `B(P,C)=r q₀+a q₁+b q₂+s q₃+q₄=0`.
   
   Since q₃=−D, q₁=−det[r b s 1], q₂=det[r a s 1], q₀=det[a b s 1], all centre/radius signs and `P₃=−2D` follow. The constant `2P₄/P₃=det[r a b s]/D` also matches. This is a correct hand derivation and, as finding 7 records, is now encoded by the supplementary certificate. It still does not derive hspan.

3. **Exact check, not a claimed random experiment.** For `(r,a,b)=(1,0,0),(2,1,0),(3,0,1),(1,2,3)`, exact rational computation yields D=8, p=(11/8,5/4), ρ=19/8. All four powers and the displayed common expression are exactly 25/16, and the corrected P is B-orthogonal to all four cycle rows.

4. **Square and finite-existence checks.** Exact computation gives rank 3 and zero cofactor vector for the actual square cycle matrix. The two displayed orthogonal-space generators have B-self-values 1 and −1. Solving the four equations for the equilateral triple plus the unit circle at (0,1) gives precisely `P=(t,0,0,0,t)`, supporting finding 9. These calculations were run in `math_review.py` using SymPy rational/symbolic arithmetic, not Lean.

5. **hspan and degeneracy.** Theorem 1 uses containment of the entire orthogonal complement in `span{A,A′}`, not an equality of subspaces and not an assumption about only null Y. The paper's actual numbered hypothesis now says this correctly. Nullness or linear independence of A,A′ is not needed by the Lean theorem. Calling its `Collinear` conclusion a unique line through the centres still requires distinct centres; coincident endpoints are allowed by the formal statement. The explicit affine weights, unlike bare `Collinear` for a set with coincident endpoints, retain meaningful content in that case.

6. **Negative results.** The counterexample values in §8 agree with the code. The completion-map distances and maximum residual agree with EXTENSION §1, and the paper explicitly labels them numerical. I did not rerun that numerical experiment: the cited `num/iterate.py` is not among the locally authorised input files, and the report alone is not a certified error-bound proof.
