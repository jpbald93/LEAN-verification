## 1. Introduction

Clifford's chain is a family of incidence theorems generated by one recursion. Fix n circles of the
plane through a common point M, in general position. Every three of them have
three second intersections, and those three lie on a generalized circle; every four of them give four
such level-3 circles, and the four share a point; and so on, each level indexed by the subfamilies of
a fixed size. The chain states that the process never dies: at every level the objects either pass
through a common point (even levels) or lie on a common circle (odd levels). The statement and its
first proofs are classical — Clifford (*Messenger of Mathematics* 5, 1870–71; reprinted in
*Mathematical Papers*, 1882), Morley (1900), Carver (1920), and the polytope picture of
Longuet-Higgins (1972); the Miquel/cross-ratio route is set out by Bobenko and Schief
(arXiv:1509.04109). The appropriate ambient plane is the inversive (Möbius) plane, in which the
"circle" of an odd level is allowed to degenerate to a line, and the "point" of an even level is
allowed to be ∞. Carver's 1920 paper *The Failure of the Clifford Chain* classifies exactly when
this happens: in the inversion plane, with one point at infinity and with circles understood to
include lines and point-circles, every improper set is a limit of proper ones and is detected by a
vanishing minor of a characteristic matrix (his §5, pp. 160–161, with Theorem 13; §6, pp. 161–164,
treats the limiting figures themselves).

**What this paper contributes is a formalisation, not new mathematics.** The claim under test is
the chain for *all* n on the Riemann sphere, with the level objects alternating between points of
`OnePoint ℂ` (∞ included) and generalized circles (lines included), together with a verification
record for the resulting Lean 4 development. The novelty scout (`SCOUT.md` §1) records the verdict
`NONE-FOUND` for the all-n statement in any proof assistant (rows L5 and L6), after searching the
Mathlib tree at pin `0df444a3` and its `docs/1000.yaml`, GitHub code search for Lean and every other
language, the Isabelle AFP, atlas-lean, lean-genius, Rocq/GeoCoq and HighSchoolGeometry, HOL Light,
HOL4, Metamath `set.mm`, the Mizar MML listing, the formal-conjectures/compfiles/ICML collections,
ProofAtlas, a numeric check of the statement, arXiv, OpenAlex, Crossref and the general web. In
Mathlib, `Clifford's circle theorems` appears in `docs/1000.yaml` with no `decl`; the Miquel results
that do exist (AFP `Miquel.thy`, 2026-08-11, Ramos–Hulak–Queiroz; atlas-lean
`MathlibExt/Geometry/Euclidean/MiquelTheorem.lean`; Rocq `HighSchoolGeometry`; lean-genius
`MiquelPivotTheoremOQ01.lean`) are all in the *pivot* or *complete-quadrilateral* form and are the
base case only. This paper therefore claims **first machine-checked treatment of the chain for
arbitrary n** — a claim scoped to the 26 search rows logged in `SCOUT.md` §0 and to the sources
§5.10 records as not searched — and nothing about the mathematics.

### 1.1 What the formalisation closes

Two statement-level targets were carried through the development:

* **C5 — the all-n chain on the sphere.** `CliffordChainS n` for every `n ≥ 1`. The library states
  this in two pieces: `cliffordChainS_of_le_five (n) (hn : n ≤ 5)` (`Clifford/Mobius.lean:178`) for
  the low levels, and the headline theorem `cliffordChainS_inf (n) (hn : 3 ≤ n)`
  (`Clifford/MorleyKernel.lean:5592`) for `n ≥ 3`. The chain of calls behind the headline is short:
  `cliffordChainS_inf` is `cliffordChainS_of_corner EvenCorner_proof`
  (`Clifford/MorleyKernel.lean:5269`), which is `cliffordChainS_of_inf'`
  (`Clifford/MorleyKernel.lean:4650`), which is the induction `cliffordChainS_all_of`
  (`Clifford/Mobius.lean:330`) instantiated at `E := EptInf` with `h4_pt_inf`
  (`Clifford/MorleyKernel.lean:2566`) as its level-4 certificate; the `n = 3` base of that induction
  comes from `cliffordChain_threeS` (`Clifford/Mobius.lean:181`). **No single declaration in the
  library states `∀ n, CliffordChainS n`**;
  the aggregate is their composition, which is stated and checked, at the pin, in
  `check/allN.lean` (`lake env lean` rc 0 in about 3 s, **std** axioms, 2026-11-24).
* **C6 — a uniform step.** The odd side and the even side are proved by separate inductions but
  from one algebraic engine: the Morley (interpolation) matrix `KP n c (conj c)` of size n×(n+1),
  whose kernel vector at level n is the Clifford object. The odd step is the payload
  `hodd_inf_all'`; the even step reduces to a single scalar identity `EvenCornerQL` in a degenerate
  corner, which was proved in round 26 (267 added lines) and promoted into the library in round 26b.

A degeneracy that had survived three earlier rounds is worth naming because it is where the work
was: on the even side every branch closed except the corner `hmid = 0 ∧ V_last = 0 ∧ P₀(d) ≠ 0`,
which forces the parent cross vector `V = 0` (from `hmid = 0` and `P₀(d) ≠ 0` alone, by
`parent_crossVec_zero_of_mid`, `Clifford/MorleyKernel.lean:4998`; the packaging lemma
`heven_inf_of_corner` additionally requires all grandchildren finite). The corner is not vacuous in
practice: a numerical search found configurations approximately in it, with the grandchildren's level
points collinear in the search (`1e-8` in the earlier, looser run, `3.9e-5` for the tuple the JSON
stores); exact membership is not claimed from that search, and the theorem below does not need it. The recorded witness has `triP` 1e-2, `cycP` 1.3e-3, transversals ≥ 1e-2 and
all `|c| ≥ 0.08`; an earlier, looser solver run had `triP` 0.14, `cycP` 0.024, transversals 0.04 (the
JSON records both runs separately; the stored tuple's `|V|` max is `4.2e-10` and its subfamily
collinearity deviation `3.9e-5`, while the `1e-9` residual and the `1e-8` collinearity figure belong
to the earlier run). That witness is **numerical evidence, not a certificate** — small residuals do
not prove the exact vanishing of `V` — and it is bundled at
`layer0/r25_jack/RANK_ROUTE_COUNTEREXAMPLE.json` (see Appendix A). It argues against the tempting
rank route; it does not refute it, and nothing below rests on it. The exact statement proved later is
stronger than collinearity: `evenCorner_of_QL`
(`Clifford/MorleyKernel.lean:5287`) and `evenCornerQL_proof` (`Clifford/MorleyKernel.lean:5539`)
show that in the exact corner all `2k+5` grandchild level points **coincide** at the single point
`−w_{2k+4}/w_{k+2}`. Round 25 reduced the corner to the scalar identity `w_last = 0` for the
subfamily cross vector `w`, and round 26 proved that identity by a polynomial argument on
dehomogenised coordinates: the row equations make `polyF := −X·Q·P̄^rev − P·Q̄^rev`, of degree
≤ 2k+5, vanish at 2k+6 pairwise non-proportional points, so it is identically zero and its top
coefficient `−W_{2k+5}·conj W_0` vanishes. The mathematics of that argument is elementary; the
formalisation is not, and it is the one place where this development goes beyond a transcription of
the classical proofs.

### 1.2 What this paper does not claim

* **The at-infinity / improper-set classification is Carver's (1920), never ours.** Carver's §5
  (pp. 160–161) proves the key-matrix criterion and its Theorem 13; §6 (pp. 161–164) treats the
  limiting figures, and it is §6 that states the inversion convention this paper's carrier is
  modelled on. The correspondence is not an identification: §6 Type 2 explicitly admits a
  **point-circle at infinity**, and Types 3/3′/6 have partially or entirely indeterminate limiting
  figures, whereas `IsGCircleS` admits finite point-circles only and excludes `{∞}`. Morley (1900)
  supplies the analytic method and the "Clifford line" phenomenon, Kantor (1878) the deltoid
  examples. In this paper these appear only as the *reason* the sphere predicate is the correct one,
  and as the citation for the ∞ case. No classification theorem is formalised here: the Lean
  development proves the chain exists, including when the level object is a line or the point at
  infinity, but it does not state the characteristic-matrix criterion in general form. **OPEN**: a
  formal version of Carver's Theorem 13 is not part of this development.
* **The mathematics is not new**: the all-n chain (Clifford 1870–71/1882; Morley 1900; Carver 1920;
  Longuet-Higgins 1972), the failure phenomena (Kantor 1878; Carver 1920), the Möbius-plane repair
  (Carver 1920 §6), the analytic method (Morley 1900) and the higher-polytope picture
  (Longuet-Higgins 1972) and the analytic, cross-ratio route (Bobenko and Schief,
  arXiv:1509.04109, published 2016) are all classical.
* **First formalisation of Miquel is also not claimed**: atlas-lean, AFP 2026, Coq HSG and
  lean-genius all contain a Miquel theorem.
* Our own earlier paper **Paper D** (Zenodo record 23653309) proves C₄ and C₅ in the *finite*
  plane unconditionally; its C₆ statement carries a further non-degeneracy hypothesis (the
  non-collinearity of selected level-4 points in every five-subfamily), so it is not the same
  predicate as this paper's `CliffordChain 6`, which §2.2 proves **false**. What is reused as base
  cases here is only the finite-plane chain for **n ≤ 5**, transported by `chainS_of_chain`; the
  all-n ≥ 6 failure of the finite-plane predicate is `not_cliffordChain_of_six_le`
  (`Clifford/Classify.lean:219`), which builds on Paper D's six-centre counterexample.

## 2. Statements

### 2.1 The carrier

Everything is stated for circles through the origin `M = 0` (a similarity moves any M there) with
centres `c : Fin n → ℂ`. The two ground-level notions are (`Clifford/Basic.lean:394`, `Clifford/Basic.lean:398`)

```lean
noncomputable def second (c d : ℂ) : ℂ := (conj c * d - c * conj d) / (conj c - conj d)

def Transversal (c d : ℂ) : Prop := conj c * d ≠ c * conj d
```

`second c d` is the second intersection of the two circles (besides `M = 0`), and `Transversal c d`
says the two circles meet transversally, equivalently that `M`, `c`, `d` are not collinear. General
position is `GenPosN` (`Clifford/Clean.lean:177`):

```lean
def GenPosN {n : ℕ} (c : Fin n → ℂ) : Prop :=
  (∀ i j, i ≠ j → Transversal (c i) (c j)) ∧
    ∀ i j k, i < j → j < k → GenTriple (c i) (c j) (c k)
```

with `GenTriple` (`Clifford/Basic.lean:402`)

```lean
def GenTriple (a b c : ℂ) : Prop :=
  triP a b c (conj a) (conj b) (conj c) ≠ 0 ∧ cycP a b c (conj a) (conj b) (conj c) ≠ 0
```

Both conjuncts matter, and the second is not a formality: by `genTriple_iff`
(`Clifford/Bridge.lean:110`) the pair says that the three centres are not collinear **and** that the
three second intersections are not collinear. The second conjunct is what guarantees that the
level-3 object is a genuine circle and never a line, which is why lines — and the point ∞ — can
first occur at levels 5 and 6, not at level 3.

The sphere carrier is defined in `Clifford/Mobius.lean`:

```lean
def lineS (p v : ℂ) : Set (OnePoint ℂ) :=
  insert ∞ (((↑) : ℂ → OnePoint ℂ) '' {z | ∃ t : ℝ, z = p + t • v})

def IsGCircleS (X : Set (OnePoint ℂ)) : Prop :=
  (∃ o : ℂ, ∃ r : ℝ, X = ((↑) : ℂ → OnePoint ℂ) '' Metric.sphere o r) ∨
    ∃ p v : ℂ, v ≠ 0 ∧ X = lineS p v
```

so the first disjunct is *any* `Metric.sphere o r` with `r` an arbitrary real: `r > 0` a genuine
circle, `r = 0` a single point (a point-circle, which the classical convention admits), `r < 0` the
empty set — which the statements never produce, since an empty set cannot contain a level point. The
second disjunct is a line of `ℂ` together with `∞`. The development carries the empty case rather
than excluding it, so the prose matches the predicate and not an idealised version of it. The chain recursion is

```lean
def ChainS : (n : ℕ) → (Fin n → ℂ) → Set (OnePoint ℂ) → Prop
  | 0, _, X => X = {((0 : ℂ) : OnePoint ℂ)}
  | 1, c, X => X = ((↑) : ℂ → OnePoint ℂ) '' Metric.sphere (c 0) ‖c 0‖
  | 2, c, X => X = {((second (c 0) (c 1) : ℂ) : OnePoint ℂ)}
  | n + 3, c, X =>
    if n % 2 = 0 then
      IsGCircleS X ∧ ∀ i : Fin (n + 3), ∃ p, ChainS (n + 2) (c ∘ i.succAbove) {p} ∧ p ∈ X
    else
      ∃ p, X = {p} ∧ ∀ i : Fin (n + 3), ∃ Y, ChainS (n + 2) (c ∘ i.succAbove) Y ∧ p ∈ Y
```

and the theorem is

```lean
def CliffordChainS (n : ℕ) : Prop := ∀ c : Fin n → ℂ, GenPosN c → ∃ X, ChainS n c X
```

`ChainS` differs from the finite-plane `Chain` (`Clifford/Clean.lean:165`) in two respects: its
carrier is `Set (OnePoint ℂ)` throughout, so the level-2 point is a point of the sphere; and at odd
levels the object must be a generalized circle *on the sphere* (`IsGCircleS`, lines containing `∞`
allowed), while even levels quantify over sphere points. The combined effect is that nothing forces a
later object to be finite.
`liftS Y` (`Clifford/Mobius.lean:51`) is the closure of a generalized circle of `ℂ` in the sphere —
the set with `∞` adjoined iff `Y` is unbounded — and `chainS_of_chain`
(`Clifford/Mobius.lean:119`) transports any finite chain object along it.

The level point is read off the kernel vector of a Morley matrix. `crossVec`
(`Clifford/MorleyKernel.lean:159`) is the vector of signed maximal minors of an n×(n+1) matrix,

```lean
def crossVec {R : Type*} [CommRing R] {n : ℕ} (A : Matrix (Fin n) (Fin (n + 1)) R) :
    Fin (n + 1) → R :=
  fun j => (-1) ^ (j : ℕ) * (A.submatrix id j.succAbove).det
```

`KP n x y` (`Clifford/MorleyKernel.lean:214`) is the row-scaled n×(n+1) Morley (interpolation)
matrix in `x = c`, `y = conj c`, with entries

```lean
def KP {R : Type*} [CommRing R] (n : ℕ) (x y : Fin n → R) : Matrix (Fin n) (Fin (n + 1)) R :=
  fun k j => if (j : ℕ) ≤ n / 2 then (-x k) ^ (j : ℕ) * y k ^ (n - n / 2 - j)
    else -((-x k) ^ ((j : ℕ) - (n / 2 + 1)) * y k ^ (n - n / 2 - 1 - ((j : ℕ) - (n / 2 + 1))))
```

It is row `k` of the polynomial matrix `Kf n (aOf c) (tOf c)` (`Clifford/MorleyKernel.lean:95`, with
`aOf c = 1 / conj c` and `tOf c = −c / conj c`, `Clifford/MorleyKernel.lean:219`) multiplied by
`(conj c k)^(n − n/2)`. For **nonzero centres** row scaling does not change the kernel
(`KP_mulVec_eq_zero_iff`, `Clifford/MorleyKernel.lean:236`); nonzero centres follow from `GenPosN` at
every level where the readout is used (`hc_of_genPos`, `Clifford/MorleyKernel.lean:2571`) but not at
`n = 1`, where `c = 0` satisfies `GenPosN` and `KP = [0, −1]` while `Kf = [1, 0]`, so the kernel
equivalence is stated with that hypothesis. The readouts are
(`Clifford/MorleyKernel.lean:452`, `Clifford/MorleyKernel.lean:2549`)

```lean
noncomputable def levelPointN (n : ℕ) (v : Fin (n + 1) → ℂ) : ℂ :=
  -v (Fin.last n) / v ⟨n / 2, by omega⟩

noncomputable def EptInf (n : ℕ) (c : Fin n → ℂ) : OnePoint ℂ :=
  if crossVec (KP n c (fun j => conj (c j))) ⟨n / 2, by omega⟩ = 0 then OnePoint.infty
  else ((levelPointN n (crossVec (KP n c (fun j => conj (c j)))) : ℂ) : OnePoint ℂ)
```

So `EptInf n c` is the projective point `[−v_last : v_{n/2}]` of `v = crossVec (KP n c c̄)`: a
vanishing middle coordinate is sent to `∞`. This includes the case of the **whole vector zero**,
which is not a projective coordinate at all; sending it to `∞` is the development's chosen
totalization, not a consequence of projective geometry. That branch is not vacuous: for the parabola
configuration of §2.4 (centres `2t − 2it²`, `t = 1, …, 6`) the middle coordinate of the level-6
parent cross vector vanishes and, by `parent_crossVec_zero_of_mid`, so does the whole vector — two
exact evaluations at the pinned tree, recorded in `REVIEW_TRIAGE_v03b.md` (**std** axioms); general
position for that tuple is proved in `check/not_UniqEven.lean`. `Counter6` itself is *not* an instance: its parent vector has last coordinate
`−238878720000`, checked exactly. The choice of totalization is therefore visible in the statements
rather than hidden.

### 2.2 Why the sphere is needed: the finite plane is false

The finite-plane predicate is

```lean
def CliffordChain (n : ℕ) : Prop := ∀ c : Fin n → ℂ, GenPosN c → ∃ X, Chain n c X
```

(`Clifford/Clean.lean:184`), and the development proves

```lean
theorem cliffordChain_iff (n : ℕ) : CliffordChain n ↔ n ≤ 5        -- Clifford/Classify.lean:227
```

by exhibiting six centres in general position with no level-6 object:
`Counter6.cx = ⟨3,−6⟩, ⟨6,−3⟩, ⟨1,2⟩, ⟨−2,−1⟩, ⟨0,−4⟩, ⟨4,0⟩` (exact Gaussian integers,
`Clifford/Counter6.lean:19`). For them every level-4 point is forced to be the Miquel point; as an
explanation rather than a quoted theorem, the six level-5 objects are lines (five distinct collinear
Miquel points each — the lines themselves are `line0`…`line5`, `Clifford/Mobius.lean:492` through
`Clifford/Mobius.lean:627`), and three of those lines are not concurrent (`Counter6.not_chain6`,
`Clifford/Counter6.lean:182`; the universal form `Counter6.not_cliffordChain_six`, `Clifford/Counter6.lean:227`). What
the proof of `not_chain6` actually uses is the contradiction from the `cD = 0` relations at three of
the six deletions (`i = 4, 5, 0`). This is Paper D's failure phenomenon, and on the sphere it
disappears rather than contradicting anything: the level-6 object is `{∞}`,

```lean
theorem chainS_six : ChainS 6 Counter6.cx {∞}                 -- Clifford/Mobius.lean:651
theorem chainS_six_unique {X : Set (OnePoint ℂ)} (h : ChainS 6 Counter6.cx X) : X = {∞}
theorem chainS_six_iff (X : Set (OnePoint ℂ)) : ChainS 6 Counter6.cx X ↔ X = {∞}
```

The pair `cliffordChain_iff` / `chainS_six_unique` is the precise sense in which the old predicate
was too small: the sphere adds the level-6 point this configuration was missing. It is *inspired by*
Carver's reading ("the plane has only one point at infinity … circle includes straight lines and
point-circles", `SCOUT.md` §0 row 18) but not identical to it — see §1.2 — and the uniqueness of
`{∞}` here is a property of this one configuration, not of the predicate (§5.4).

### 2.3 The odd side is hypothesis-free

For odd levels the payload is an existence statement for a generalized circle through the canonical
level-(n−1) points, and it holds for every `GenPosN` configuration with no side conditions
(`Clifford/MorleyKernel.lean:4643`):

```lean
theorem hodd_inf_all' :
    ∀ (k : ℕ) (d : Fin (2 * k + 5) → ℂ), GenPosN d →
      ∃ Y, Clifford.IsGCircleS Y ∧
        ∀ i : Fin (2 * k + 5), EptInf (2 * k + 4) (d ∘ i.succAbove) ∈ Y
```

It is obtained from the three-case split `hodd_inf_P0_eq` / `hodd_inf_P0_ne` /
`hodd_inf_P0_ne_some` on `P₀`. Note what the statement *says*: it names the canonical point
`EptInf (2k+4) (d ∘ i.succAbove)` rather than asserting that some point of that level lies on `Y`.
What turns these named readouts into actual chain objects is a separate step: `h4_pt_inf`
(`Clifford/MorleyKernel.lean:2566`) certifies the level-4 case (`ChainS 4 c {EptInf 4 c}` under
`GenPosN`), and `cliffordChainS_all_of` (`Clifford/Mobius.lean:330`) is the two-step induction that
rebuilds `ChainS (2k+4) c {EptInf (2k+4) c}` from that base case plus the odd and even payloads. A
reader who wants the chain itself must take the three together; §5.3 records exactly what the
layer-13 comparison did with the payloads. That the odd side needs no hypothesis beyond general
position is a statement about the development, not about the mathematics: Carver's improper sets
exist on the odd side too, and the reason we can state the odd side cleanly is that `IsGCircleS`
admits the line as a legitimate level object.

### 2.4 The even side, and the corner

On the even side the deliverable is: for every configuration of `2k+6` circles and every choice `i`
of parent object — the subfamily obtained by deleting `c i`, whose object sits at level `2k+5`
(level 5 only when `k = 0`) — there is a generalized circle `Y` through the canonical level-(2k+6)
point which contains all `2k+5` canonical level-(2k+4) points of the sub-configurations. Written out
(`Clifford/MorleyKernel.lean:5586`):

```lean
theorem heven_inf_all :
    ∀ (k : ℕ) (c : Fin (2*k+6) → ℂ), GenPosN c → ∀ i : Fin (2*k+6),
      ∃ Y, Clifford.IsGCircleS Y ∧ EptInf (2*k+6) c ∈ Y ∧
        ∀ j : Fin (2*k+5), EptInf (2*k+4) ((c ∘ i.succAbove) ∘ j.succAbove) ∈ Y
```

As in §2.3, the theorem names the canonical points rather than saying "some point of the level", and
again the named readouts are certified as chain objects through `h4_pt_inf` and the induction — not
by the payload alone. The proof splits four ways; three branches are unconditional: `P₀(d) = 0`
(`heven_line_of_P0`), `hmid ≠ 0` with all children finite (`heven_kernel`), and some child
`P_{k+2} = 0` (`children_eq_of_P3_zero`: there the **finite** child points coincide; a child with a
vanishing middle coordinate is `∞` by `EptInf`, and `heven_of_common` builds a line through the common
finite point, any infinite children and the parent — no non-uniqueness is inferred in this branch). The fourth is the corner. It is named twice, first as the geometric statement
(`Clifford/MorleyKernel.lean:5253`)

```lean
def EvenCorner : Prop :=
  ∀ (k : ℕ) (c : Fin (2*k+6) → ℂ), GenPosN c → ∀ i : Fin (2*k+6),
    crossVec (KP (2*k+6) c (fun j => conj (c j))) = 0 →
    crossVec (KP (2*k+5) (c ∘ i.succAbove) (fun r => conj ((c ∘ i.succAbove) r))) 0 ≠ 0 →
    (∀ j : Fin (2*k+5), crossVec (KP (2*k+4) ((c ∘ i.succAbove) ∘ j.succAbove)
        (fun r => conj (((c ∘ i.succAbove) ∘ j.succAbove) r))) ⟨k+2, by omega⟩ ≠ 0) →
    ∃ p u : ℂ, u ≠ 0 ∧ ∀ j : Fin (2*k+5), ∃ t : ℝ,
      levelPointN (2*k+4) (crossVec (KP (2*k+4) ((c ∘ i.succAbove) ∘ j.succAbove)
        (fun r => conj (((c ∘ i.succAbove) ∘ j.succAbove) r)))) = p + (t : ℂ) * u
```

and then, after round 25, as the scalar identity that implies it (`Clifford/MorleyKernel.lean:5317`):

```lean
def EvenCornerQL : Prop := … (same hypotheses) …
    crossVec (KP (2*k+5) (c ∘ i.succAbove) (fun r => conj ((c ∘ i.succAbove) r))) (Fin.last _) = 0

theorem evenCorner_of_QL …                                     -- Clifford/MorleyKernel.lean:5287
theorem evenCorner_of_cornerQL (hQ : EvenCornerQL) : EvenCorner  -- Clifford/MorleyKernel.lean:5325
```

One property of `EvenCornerQL` is worth stating because it looks like a gap and is not one: the
grandchild-finiteness hypothesis (the third premise, `hall`) is never used by `evenCornerQL_proof`
(`Clifford/MorleyKernel.lean:5539` introduces it as `_hall`), so the identity holds without it. Layer
6 could not flag it, because `EvenCornerQL` bundles its hypotheses inside a `Prop` definition rather
than as top-level binders, and the ablation pass only sees binders. `hall` *is* used downstream, by
`evenCorner_of_QL`, to deduce `EvenCorner`.

Round 26 proved `EvenCornerQL` itself (`evenCornerQL_proof`, `Clifford/MorleyKernel.lean:5539`, +267 lines over the round-25
prefix, which is byte-identical on its first 5344 lines **in the scratch file**
`scratch/ChainEvenCornerQL.lean`; the promoted file's lines 1006–2005 differ from it by the gate edits
of §4), and the chain follows with no hypotheses left:

```lean
theorem EvenCorner_proof : EvenCorner                       -- Clifford/MorleyKernel.lean:5584
theorem heven_inf_all : …                                   -- Clifford/MorleyKernel.lean:5586
theorem cliffordChainS_inf (n : ℕ) (hn : 3 ≤ n) : CliffordChainS n   -- Clifford/MorleyKernel.lean:5592
```

**The even-level point is not unique, and no uniqueness theorem is available.** `EptInf` selects one
canonical point per level, but `ChainS` is existential at every level, and in degenerate
configurations it admits more than one even-level object. The counterexample checked for this draft
(`check/not_UniqEven.lean`, `lake env lean` rc 0 in about 28 s, **std** axioms, 2026-11-24) takes the six
centres `2t − 2it²`, `t = 1..6`: `GenPosN` holds, all fifteen four-subfamily Miquel points are `4i`,
every five-subfamily admits the line `lineS (4i) 1`, and that line contains both `4i` and `∞`, so
both `{4i}` and `{∞}` satisfy `ChainS 6`. Hence

```lean
theorem not_UniqEven : ¬ (∀ (k : ℕ) (d : Fin (2*k+4) → ℂ), GenPosN d → ∀ p : OnePoint ℂ,
    ChainS (2*k+4) d {p} → p = EptInf (2*k+4) d)
```

A uniqueness statement of that form is false under the current definitions; any future one needs
extra hypotheses or a different selection predicate. What the development supplies is a choice of
canonical points (`EptInf`) together with the existential chain objects — not a unique classical or
limiting object in degenerate cases. §5.3 and §5.4 record what this does and does not settle about
the prose.

### 2.5 The theorem

```lean
theorem cliffordChainS_inf (n : ℕ) (hn : 3 ≤ n) : CliffordChainS n
```

with the levels `n ≤ 5` supplied by `cliffordChainS_of_le_five` (`Clifford/Mobius.lean:178`), which
derives them from the finite-plane theorem through `cliffordChainS_of_cliffordChain`
(`Clifford/Mobius.lean:157`) and the transport `chainS_of_chain` (`Clifford/Mobius.lean:119`);
`chainS_of_chain` and its singleton form `chainS_of_chain_pt` (`Clifford/Mobius.lean:152`) are the
two general transport implications, and it is `cliffordChainS_of_le_five` — not the transports —
that gives the sphere base cases. Inside `cliffordChainS_inf`, `n = 3` uses `cliffordChain_threeS`
(`Clifford/Mobius.lean:181`) and `n = 4` uses `h4_pt_inf`, both transported. The aggregate claim for
every `n ≥ 1` is the composition of the two, `check/allN.lean` (§1.1).

Read out in prose, for every `n ≥ 1` and every `GenPosN` family of n circles through M: a Clifford
object of level n exists on the Riemann sphere; for odd `n ≥ 3` it is a generalized circle
containing, for each `i`, some level-(n−1) object of the family with `c i` deleted; for even
`n ≥ 4` it is a point (possibly ∞) lying on, for each `i`, some level-(n−1) object of that family.
The indefinite article is forced by `ChainS`: the recursion quantifies existentially at every level,
so neither the lower-level objects nor the even-level point are determined in general (§5.4). All
four terminal theorems — `evenCornerQL_proof`, `EvenCorner_proof`, `heven_inf_all`,
`cliffordChainS_inf` — depend on **std** only, as printed by `#print axioms` at the end of the file
and re-checked by the gate.
